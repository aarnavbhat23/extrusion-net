# Extrusion submission path — 2026-11-01

Decision: keep Extrusion separate from spatial and maintain a bounded theory
track. The present assessment is approximately 7/10 overall with roughly
one-point judgment uncertainty. The target is a defensible 7.5-8 research paper;
this plan does not earn a new grade or establish acceptance odds.

Working title: **What normalized kinetic snapshots conceal about intervention predictions**.

The strongest available paper candidate is the loss of intervention information
under a specific observation contract, with explicit recovery alternatives and
finite-data limitations. [Normalized snapshot theory](NORMALIZED_SNAPSHOT_THEORY.md)
and the [finite-loss study](FINITE_LOSS_RESULTS.md) support that story. The
passive-memory work is a separate exploratory direction and should not become
an additional headline in the same paper.

## What is already strong and what remains weak

| Component | Current evidence | Submission requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Observation ambiguity | Explicit normalized-resolvent family with identical retained-state laws and differing intervention targets | Independent proof check and equation-level priority review; distinguish a substantive application result from elementary algebra |
| Minimal recovery measurements | Exact recovery alternatives for the specified capture family | State precisely what remains identifiable with uncertain loss, loading and perturbation strength; do not generalize the inverse to arbitrary hidden generators |
| Practical comparison | Frozen studies retain strong timestamp/absolute-retention controls and failed normalized-design gates | Useful certified intervention intervals, or a rigorous demonstration of their intrinsic failure; point errors and optimizer convergence are insufficient |
| Biological transfer | Measured iFRAP/encounter candidates fail strong-control gates | A new biology/architecture claim needs a justified observation operator and held-out perturbation; current data cannot be presented as successful transfer |

## Ordered work and stop decisions

1. **Choose one intervention target and uncertainty class.** Keep the already
   declared removal target and observation contracts. Bound intervention strength,
   loading and observation loss explicitly. Distinguish errors in those inputs
   from ordinary sampling error. Do not tune their bounds on the target response.
2. **Finish target-specific identified sets.** Derive attainable lower/upper
   intervention predictions and construct mechanisms attaining them when possible.
   Where exact bounds are unavailable, distinguish certified outer bounds from
   feasible inner witnesses. Another point inverse is not the missing contribution.
3. **Construct useful finite-sample predictive uncertainty.** Reuse established
   likelihood/confidence tools as baselines. Certify the intervention image of the
   uncertainty set; retain unbounded or wide cases. Numerical optimization alone
   cannot claim certified global extrema. If precision cannot be useful, consider
   a focused impossibility/design paper only if the boundary is substantial and
   original enough after review.
4. **Freeze a fair comparison.** Compare retained absences, scheduled timestamps,
   calibrated optical/state references and the proposed minimal design under the
   same declared resource budget. Report which methods have extra denominators,
   observations or calibration. Keep simple controls that win. Use fresh mechanisms
   and independent implementation checks; same-model simulation is not biology.
5. **External validation matched to the claim.** A conditional theory paper can
   validate equations and conclusions with independent simulators/known references
   while leaving biology open. A biological prediction paper additionally requires
   independent measurement calibration and an untouched perturbation cohort.
   Existing negative measured-data results must retain their status.

An original target-specific identification boundary plus useful calibrated
prediction would make a 7.5-8 assessment plausible. A chain of elementary identities
and more matched simulations would not. If the new identified-set problem is
already solved in the closest literature or yields no useful conclusion, keep a
technical note/resource and postpone a full methods paper.

## Keep the nature-inspired exploration bounded

[Passive deformation memory](PASSIVE_MEMORY_DIRECTIONS.md) is worth studying only
after specifying which reference data can bound unresolved strain and slow modes.
The nine counterexamples already show that different futures after equal current
observations need not indicate ongoing activity. Existing
[BILD/Rouse inference](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9069445/) and
[predictive-state representations](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2001/hash/1e4d36177d71bbb3558e43af9577d70e-Abstract.html)
are established controls. Neither another hidden-state name nor a generic
memory-sensitive rule supplies architecture novelty. If this exploration obtains
a distinct result, evaluate it as a separate future paper.

## Paper and release shape

Use four or five figures: identical calibration/different intervention example;
identification boundary; predictive uncertainty versus budget; comparison with
timestamps/retention; external reference only if its claim is justified. Keep
unrelated sequence-learning and memory variants out of the main story while
preserving their archived outcomes. Assemble the claim/figure skeleton now;
write the full manuscript after the central missing result is established.

Submission additionally requires independent mathematical feedback, complete
proof/priority comparison, reproducible figures from immutable outputs, clear
source licenses and all venue-required disclosures. No referee feedback, new
validation, paper submission or new model claim is implied by this plan.
