# Transfer beyond the static product generator

Exploratory structural-transfer screen, not real-world validation. Keep the
recovery algorithm frozen. Generate responses from independently enumerated arm
reach distributions and finite lifetimes rather than the production contact sum.

Compare two lifetime models. Independent geometric lifetimes for the two arms
are a matched control, equivalent to multiplying each pass gate by survival s.
A single shared geometric lifetime couples the two arms through the maximum
time needed to reach their endpoints; it need not belong to the static product
family. Finite motor lifetime is an established biological modeling ingredient.

Use five mechanisms each at 16/32 loads, original gates uniform [.2,.6], s in
1/.95/.8/.5, output noise SD 0/.0001/.001. Use the original recovery domain [.0,.6],
analytical color selection and its same projected iteration. Match noise across
the two lifetime models. Validate on an uncut and a single right-cut response
at held-out uniform-.5 loading, with a constant value vector. Split alpha .05
equally between calibration and held-out noise bounds. Report recovery of the
effective marginal gates, held-out prediction error and rejection frequency.
Do not interpret recovery of an effective gate as recovery of raw kinetic gates.

As a mechanistic diagnostic only, record whether excess distance-three contacts
relative to the static model give a consistent shared-lifetime signature after
two-view adjacent-contact inversion. These extra full-map bands are not part of
the original vector-response budget and cannot silently repair a failed method.

Seed 20261113 is an arbitrary identifier. Continue beyond the theorem only if
the mechanism passes the held-out tests or the violation is detected. A positive
matched control alone does not establish generality. Retain every mismatch.
